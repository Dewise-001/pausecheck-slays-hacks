# PauseCheck sources

- **Training corpus:** [UCI Machine Learning Repository — SMS Spam Collection](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/ml/datasets/sms+spam+collection). UCI reports 5,574 labeled messages; the corpus combines manually collected UK spam reports with legitimate-message subsets from Singapore-related and thesis sources, plus an older public spam corpus. Its repository page identifies classification as an associated task and gives the **CC BY 4.0** license. The training script case-folds and whitespace-normalizes text before deduplication, drops contradictory-label text groups before a fixed stratified 70/15/15 train/validation/test split, and exports learned parameters plus aggregate evaluation only; it does not package raw SMS text. The current corpus leaves 5,159 messages after deduplication, with no contradictory-label groups. Citation: Almeida, T. & Hidalgo, J. (2011). *SMS Spam Collection* [Dataset]. UCI Machine Learning Repository. <https://doi.org/10.24432/C5CC84>.
- **Exploratory Nigerian-context stress-test source:** [Eterniti/Xvimo team, NaijaScamBench v0](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Eterniti/NaijaScamBench), CC BY 4.0. Its data card describes 180 labeled messages: 135 constructed by the Xvimo team and 45 legitimate messages sourced from UCI. The private test evaluated the frozen PauseCheck model only on the 135 constructed examples and excluded the UCI rows to avoid overlap with the model’s training source. The card warns that the local-language examples have not been reviewed by native speakers and that constructed examples can be optimistic. It is not real-world validation. The exact revision and CSV checksum, aggregate metrics and limitations are recorded in `LOCAL_PILOT_EVALUATION.json` and `LOCAL_PILOT.md`.
- **Candidate corpus not used:** [Nigerian Computer-Mediated Fraudulent Text Messages](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16947613), CC BY 4.0. The Zenodo record describes collection from researchers’ personal inboxes, colleagues and students, but does not describe message-level de-identification or privacy safeguards. It was not downloaded or used; an open license alone is not sufficient evidence that private message content is safe to reuse.
- **Nigerian phishing guidance:** [Nigeria Computer Emergency Response Team (ngCERT), “Increase in Phishing Campaigns within the Nigerian Cyberspace”](https://cert.gov.ng/advisories/increase-in-phishing-campaigns-within-the-nigerian-cyberspace), published January 15, 2025 (advisory ID ngCERT-2025-010001). The opened advisory describes deceptive SMS, WhatsApp, email and social messages impersonating organisations; links to fake sites and urgent financial-aid claims; and recommends verifying schemes through official sources, avoiding unexpected links/attachments, not sharing personal or financial information with unverified parties, and reporting suspicious activity to ngCERT.
- **Consumer protection:** [Central Bank of Nigeria — Fraud and Scam Awareness](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/supervision/cpdfraudandscam.html). CBN advises users not to click suspicious links in messages, to verify senders by contacting organisations directly, and to be cautious with requests for personal or financial information. It also recommends contacting one’s financial institution promptly if banking information is compromised.

These sources inform app copy and data attribution; they do **not** establish that PauseCheck is approved by these organisations or validated on Nigerian messages. The model's held-out test metrics in `model-evaluation.json` are limited to the UCI corpus.
